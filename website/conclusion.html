Conclusion & Future work

  We built a traffic sign recognition system based on a convolutional neural network trained on the GTSRB dataset. The model sorts an image into one of 43 sign types and correctly recognises 95.1% of the 12,630 test images, with an average precision of 92.9% and recall of 92.8% across the classes. It trains in minutes on an ordinary CPU, which shows that a small CNN is enough to recognise cropped traffic sign images accurately.

  The system has clear limits. It recognises signs but does not find them: it works best when the picture shows a single sign filling most of the frame, as in the dataset, and it cannot locate signs in a wider street scene. When the model is unsure it reports that no sign was recognised, but it can still be confidently wrong on photos with no sign in them. Most mistakes are between signs that look alike at 30x30 pixels, such as the 60 and 80 km/h speed limits, and accuracy is lower for rarer classes such as Pedestrians and for rotated or blurred images.

  Future work: add a detection stage (for example YOLO or Faster R-CNN) that finds signs in full camera frames before classifying them; train with data augmentation (small rotations, brightness and blur changes) and class balancing to make the model more robust and improve the weaker classes; and extend the system to video from a vehicle-mounted camera for real-time recognition.
